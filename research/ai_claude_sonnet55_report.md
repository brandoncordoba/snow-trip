# White Christmas trip: research report

**Read this first:** the WebSearch and WebFetch tools were not permitted in this run, so I used curl only. Most tourism and resort pages are JavaScript-rendered and returned no prices or snow data. I did read a few real facts: Red River's elevation (8,681 ft), the Rocky Mountain National Park entrance fee ($15-$35), and a Winter Park lodging listing that showed "from $389/night" with no date attached. **Nearly every other number in destinations.json is an estimate from general knowledge**, listed per destination in `estimated_fields`. Treat all dollar figures as planning ranges and re-price on Google Flights and the lodging sites before booking.

## Ranked top 3 for this family

1. **Winter Park, Colorado (top pick).** It is about 1.75 hours from Denver, with a lift-served tubing hill, a kids' ski and ride lesson, the Cabriolet and gondola, and reliable snow at both dates. I estimate 90% odds of 1 inch or more of snow on the ground on Dec 25. The risk is altitude (about 9,050 ft in town, higher on the mountain), which can bother small kids on day one.
2. **Red River, New Mexico.** It is the only destination within 14 driving hours of Conroe (about 13.5 hours, an estimate), so there are no airfares. The town sits at 8,681 ft (read from redriver.org). The town site lists tubing, sledding, snowshoeing, a winter constellations hike, and hot cocoa and s'mores. Lodging is much cheaper than in Colorado's big resorts. The risks are the long drive with kids and the altitude.
3. **Estes Park / Rocky Mountain National Park, Colorado.** It is cheap, has elk, and is at lower altitude (about 7,500 ft). It is the least snow-certain of the three, because town snow in December is thin, so the park is the snow plan. Trail Ridge Road is closed in winter.

Others researched: Breckenridge, Steamboat Springs, Pagosa Springs, Jackson Hole, and Banff. Jackson and Banff have the most spectacular scenery. Both cost far more to reach, and Banff needs passports.

## Christmas week versus a February week: the verdict

**Recommendation: go in February (Feb 1-6, 2027).** Mom is right on cost, and Dad's Colorado idea still works.

For the top pick, Winter Park (all figures are estimates):

| Item | Christmas week | Early February | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lodging, 5 nights | 5 x $450 = $2,250 | 5 x $250 = $1,250 | about $1,000 |
| Flights, 4 people | 4 x $520 = $2,080 | 4 x $300 = $1,200 | about $880 |
| **Total** | **$4,330** | **$2,450** | **about $1,880 saved** |

February also gives deeper base snow, with roughly 40 inches at the base against 20 in late December. The lift lines are shorter too. The cost is that it is colder at night and the family loses the Christmas morning atmosphere. If Dad wants Christmas itself, Red River is the low-cost option. Driving avoids about $2,000 of airfare, and the family can open presents at home first.

## Anything surprising

- Early-season snow is the real Christmas risk. Lower towns such as Estes Park and Pagosa Springs have thin snow cover in late December, which would break the "white Christmas" goal. Resort bases at 9,000 ft and above are much more reliable.
- The Winter Park lodging page showed a "from $389/night" example. I could not tell which dates it was for.
- The national park fee was lower than I expected: $15-$35 per vehicle.
